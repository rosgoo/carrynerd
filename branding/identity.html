gearherd identity working doc · internal

Identity working doc

Every candidate name and mark in one place, rendered at the sizes they actually have to survive. Marks are drawn on the same 32×32 grid in the same construction grammar — thin curved slivers for horn, hard corners for mass, one flat fill, no strokes on the silhouette — so they are comparable rather than merely different. Palette is imported verbatim from src/styles/app.css.

Not linked from the site and not in public/, so it never ships. Open it straight off disk. Toggle the theme — a mark that only works on dark grey is not finished.

01  Current mark

gearherd
on --raised, ink
on the white image plate
reversed out of accent

One defect, worth fixing before this goes any further. The muzzle notch is painted var(--bg) rather than cut out of the silhouette. On --bg it is invisible, which is why it has never shown up — but look at the two lower swatches on the right: on the white image plate and reversed out of the accent, the notch fills with dark grey and the head loses its muzzle. It will do the same on any product photo, in any email client that ignores custom properties, and in a single-colour favicon. The fix is one line and does not change the drawing at all — merge the notch into the body path and set fill-rule="evenodd", so it becomes a real hole. That variant is #m-bison-eo, second in the grid below.

02  Candidates

gearherd
current

The American icon. Hump-plus-low-horn is one of the most legible profiles there is, and it is the only animal here that reads at 14px without squinting. Cost of that legibility: it is everywhere — state seals, banks, breweries, at least three outdoor brands. You will never own it.

★ incumbent · fix the notch, then keep
gearherd
drop-in

Same bison, pixel for pixel, with the muzzle cut out instead of painted over. Works reversed, works on photography, works as a one-colour favicon. There is no argument for shipping the other one.

ship this instead
calipered
previous

What the project shipped under before the rename, and still the most distinctive mark we have — nobody else in this category has a caliper. It says measured, spec-sheet, comparison, which is literally what the site does. What it does not say is herd, so it cannot follow the new name.

superseded · keep the aesthetic, not the mark
muskox
strongest alt

Same weight and cold-durability read as the bison with almost no competition — the horn boss is a helmet, which is a better metaphor for gear than a hump. And musk oxen circle up around the young when threatened, which is an actual brand story rather than a vibe. Risk: the boss-plus-hook silhouette is the first thing to go soft below 16px.

live option · needs a 14px pass
bighorn
best shape

The curl is the best single shape in the animal kingdom for a logo, and drawn as two exact 240° arcs it inherits the drafted, instrument-made quality the caliper had — it is the only mark here a compass could reproduce. The name is the problem, not the mark: Bighorn is taken hard — outfitters, a Ram trim level, a dozen gun brands. Worth stealing the mark for another name.

mark yes · name probably not
aurochs
widest

Extinct wild ox, ancestor of every cow alive, hunted out in 1627 — mythic in a way the others aren't, and nobody in gear is using it. Two problems: the lyre horns want horizontal room that a square favicon does not have, and roughly nobody can pronounce or spell it on first contact.

park it · great story, bad ergonomics
gearherd
incumbent header

No animal. This is what .brand i already renders in the header today — an 11px accent square. Zero drawing risk, perfectly on-brand for a spec-sheet aesthetic, and it never mushes. It is also completely anonymous, and the name is doing all the work.

fallback only

03  Name shortlist

NameCollective nounReadRisk
gearherdherd — bison, correct Says exactly what the site is: a herd of gear. Compound reads instantly, one word, lowercase sets well in mono. Low. "Herd/heard" homophone is a mild spoken-word tax.
muskoxherd — correct Short, hard consonants, obviously an animal. Loses the explicit gear reference. Low. Barely used in this category.
bighornherd — correct Rugged, familiar, and the mark is the best of the set. High. Heavily contested — outfitters, trucks, firearms.
aurochsherd — correct Mythic and entirely unclaimed. Extinct-animal names age well. High. Unspellable on hearing; wide mark fights a square icon.
drovedrove — driven cattle/oxen Short, real collective noun, quietly implies movement and volume. High. Collides head-on with the past tense of "drive".
soundersounder — wild boar Great word, great animal. Disqualified — a sounder is not a herd, and "sounder" reads as audio.
calipered— The previous name. Precise, technical, matched the whole visual system. Retired. No herd, no animal, past-tense verb as a noun.

Herd animals considered and cut: elk and stag (antlers are gorgeous and utterly saturated in outdoor branding), longhorn (great symmetric mark, reads as Texas first and gear second), yak (best gear-hauling story of any animal here, weakest silhouette), caribou (owned by a coffee chain), wildebeest (the collective noun is a confusion, which is funny and off-message), gaur, water buffalo, ox. Miscast as herd animals: wild boar travel in a sounder, and moose are essentially solitary — both get used for herd branding constantly and both are wrong.

05  Rules and open questions

  • The 14px test comes first. The header renders the mark at 14px and the favicon at 16px. Anything that does not survive there is decoration, not identity. Judge every candidate in the .tiny strip above before looking at the 46px version.
  • One flat fill, no strokes on the silhouette. Strokes scale badly and hairlines disappear at favicon size. The bighorn arcs are the one sanctioned exception, and only because uniform-width arcs are exactly what a stroke is good at.
  • No knockouts in var(--bg). Cut holes with fill-rule="evenodd" instead. See section 01 — this is the live bug.
  • The mark inherits currentColor. .brand svg sets color: var(--accent); nothing inside the mark should hardcode a colour.
  • Open: does the wordmark keep the caliper-era letterspacing, or tighten now that the name is a compound? Currently untouched.
  • Open: if the bison stays, is there anything left of the caliper worth keeping — a beam rule under the wordmark, say — or does the rename mean a clean break?
  • Open: the favicon at public/favicon.svg is modified in the working tree and has not been reconciled with whatever ships in Logo.astro. They must be the same drawing.
